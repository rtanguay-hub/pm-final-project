## Finalized product hypothesis

> Based on Mood-Driven Seekers who can't turn a feeling into a title (a search for "slow quiet French drama" returns *Fast & Furious*, UXR-07; users close the app after 20 minutes without watching, UXR-01) and a **7-point drop in searches that end in a play (41% → 34%)**, I believe that letting **Mood-Driven Seekers (Wanderers)** search and browse by mood will result in **higher retention**, as measured by **recovering search-to-play conversion to 41%** and a **+5 pt lift in Month 1 retention (79% → 84%)**. I will protect **Power User sessions per week (4.8×, no drop of more than 5%)** and make a go/no-go decision after **90 days**, with guardrail checkpoints at days 30 and 60.

## Success metrics

| Metric | Type | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 retention for new cohorts exposed to mood discovery | North-star | 79% → 84% (+5 pts over Spotlight cohorts) | Retention is the business outcome. Mo. 0→1 is where cohorts lose the most users (Full Library cohorts lost 32–37%), and discovery improvements have already moved it (Spotlight: 63% → 79%). |
| Content searched then played | Leading indicator | 34% → 41% (back to the level of 6 months ago) | Directly measures the friction: users search and fail to find. It's the first number that should move if mood matching works. Also watch sessions reaching 30+ min (11% → 15%) to confirm plays are real matches, not samples that get abandoned. |
| Power User sessions per week | Guardrail | Stays at or above 4.6× (no drop of more than 5% from 4.8×) | Power Users are 22% of the base at the highest LTV ($14.20), so mood discovery must not crowd out the curated content they rely on. Also watch Spotlight engagement (at or above 18%), since Spotlight already cuts Wanderer churn by 22 pts. |
